## The peer skill, its paragraph on handing over (drafted by designer 1 on 2026-11-16 to the owner's order, the seventeenth entry of the rulings file; not cold-read)

Replaces the paragraph that begins "When the context hook gives its notice, start your successor":

> When the context hook gives its notice, wrap up: bring what is in hand to a point where it can rest, bring the notes to their format and drain them of everything else, and write the owner a handoff note that says what is settled, what is in hand, what comes next and the successor's prompt. When the owner has approved the note, start your successor: a new session on Fable 5.2 at xhigh effort, titled as you are with the next number, whose prompt is the approved one with one line added, saying that it succeeds you, that its first message goes to you, by your session id, and that it waits for your answer before it writes to the owner. Check in its session record that it started on that model and effort; if not, archive it and start again. Ground it: ask it what the owner would ask about the work, check each answer against the files, and correct it until its answers hold. Then tell the owner it is grounded, put "[retired]" before your own session's title, and stop: if the owner writes to you after that, pass the words on to your successor and tell the owner it answers there.

## A preference, on questions (drafted by designer 1 on 2026-11-16 to the owner's order, the seventeenth entry; not cold-read)

New in `.claude/OWNER_PREFERENCES.md`. It replaces the line peer 2 showed and never wrote, "A question from the owner is answered before any work that has not started, except the reading or fetching the answer needs; what already runs keeps running."

> A question from the owner comes first. Where it needs discussion and not a one-off answer, the peer answers it and starts no further action until the discussion is settled. Where it is a simple question with a one-shot answer, about what is settled, the state of things or a fact, the peer answers it first and then goes on with what it was doing. In both cases what is already running keeps running, and the peer may read, fetch or check what a grounded answer needs.
