## (3) A tail kept under both readings, in a tree that reproduces the source

The tree proposed in the section on the tree, below, is a dense column of typed nodes: each node is one variant of one enum, with its parts as named fields, and the token table reproduces the source. Under it:

- A body line is one node, `Line { head, tail }`: `head` is the `Placement` up to the colon (kind, flags, name, default, gate, attributes), the same head for a node line and a wire line, since §7.1's `Placement` and §8.1's `WirePlacement` differ only after the colon; `tail` is one of `None` (the line ends), `Block(items)`, `Children(members)`, `Destination(expr)`, `Name(id)` (one bare or qualified name, the ruling's neutral node) or `Both { children, destination }`. A view finds each by its role, a field (1.8), never by a position.
- `Both` holds two readings of one token range. The source is reproduced from the token table, whose starts tile the text; the tree's part of that check is that each node's span lies inside its parent's and that siblings' spans are in order and do not overlap, with one stated exception: the two readings of a `Both` tail cover the same span, and the check walks the children reading. Each node's span, its first and end token, is a parallel column written with the node.
- A tail that fits neither reading is a syntax fault. Both readings run over the tail. Where the fault stands and what it says when a tail fits neither reading is owed to the §16 batch (the notes), so this is a reading for the batch to confirm, not a decision here: §16.3's "first token that continues no production" is the later of the two tokens at which each reading stopped, since each reading stops at the first token it cannot continue; its region is §16.3's, "the rest of its line and the block indented under that line". The tree keeps `Line { head, tail: Fault(f) }`: the head stands, so §16.3's "An item's name, when it stands before the fault, is declared" holds by reading the head, and the `Fault` node, with its code and its token, spans the region, so the region's tokens are owned and the source is reproduced. A fault inside the head (`Scene dusk | hold=:`) stands in the role where the value would (`attrs: [Entry { name: hold, value: Fault }]`) and the roles after it on the line are empty, swallowed by the region. The later stage makes a placeholder of every item whose line holds a `Fault` (§16.3: "Every item its line writes … is a placeholder"), a question answered once beside the enum: whether a node holds a fault, by a walk of its line.
- Not every syntax fault is a production's: §16.3, "A fault of §2.2 to §2.6's rules or of a production is a syntax fault." A tab in leading whitespace, a CR with no LF, a byte order mark, a semicolon, `==`, a stray `^`, a line whose indentation lands on no column, and a block under a line that opens none have no production and no `Line { head, tail }` to sit in. Their home: the lexer writes a fault token, with its code, into the token table where it found the fault; the parser, meeting one, makes a `Fault` node where it stands, inside a construct in the role it interrupts, and at a line's start as a `Fault` item, an alternative of the body's items, the top level's items and a pattern's items, spanning the region §16.3 gives it (for a mis-indented line, "every following line indented at or deeper than its column, up to the first line indented less"). So every fault of the stage is a node, and 1.5 holds for the lexer's faults as for the grammar's.
- What a reading that fails leaves: the nodes it had completed stay in the column, under no root, as the standard's example leaves them ("A faulty expression leaves the nodes it had completed in the column, under no root"). They are never read: the only ways into the column are the root's roles. They cost rows, at most the tokens of the failed reading, since each token completes at most one node of it, so the growth row "nodes a token" bounds them. Removing them would shrink the column, against 1.12's "a table never shrinks" and 1.1's "an entry once written never changes"; copying a construct's nodes into the column only on its completion would cost a copy a node (2.2). Two choices for the owner, below: leave them unreachable (the pick), or make them reachable from the `Fault` node as its abandoned parts, which §16.3's "The region is not read" argues against.

## (4) A top-level `Kind name: rest`

§7.1: `TopPlacement ::= Kind Flags? 'as'? Name DefaultArg? Gate? Attrs? (NEWLINE | Body)`, and "a wire at top level is an error". `TopPlacement` has no destination alternative, so at top level the position settles what a body line's tail leaves open: the tail is read by the same function as a body line's (1.4), with the destination reading off, and a neutral `Name` is a child, decided by the position, which 1.9 allows ("at the first stage whose listed inputs settle it"). A tail that fits no children reading is a syntax fault at the first token that continues no production of `TopPlacement` (`Lamp stage: if a then b else c` faults at `if`; `Lamp stage: Lamp/0.5 + 1` at `+`), with the catalogue entry proposed on the page: a top-level line's colon takes children or a block, and a wire stands only in a body. A top-level line whose tail is children or a block hands on a `TopPlacement` node; whether its kind names a wire kind is the later stage's, which draws §7.1's error then (`Triggers t: night` parses as a `Triggers` holding `night`, and the later stage refuses it as a wire at top level; the parser knows no names). One fault, one message: a line the parser faults has its kind unresolved (§16.3: "no other name on the line is resolved"), so the later error does not draw for it.

The other option, reading a top-level tail under both readings and refusing a destination-only tail by name as "a wire at top level": a better message for `Triggers t: if …`, a wrong one for `Lamp stage: Lamp/0.5 + 1`, and a second reading of the tail for no decision the position has not already made.

## (5) The parser's inputs, and the five scans

**Inputs.** One file's text and nothing else. Not the vocabulary: the colon ruling makes every name-dependent reading neutral, and a unit suffix is lexical, §2.6's `UnitSuffix ::= Name` with "A suffix follows a decimal literal with no whitespace" and "In a decimal literal, `e` or `E` starts an exponent only when a digit, or a sign and a digit, follows it; otherwise it starts a suffix", so `200ms` is one number token whose suffix the later stage checks against the vocabulary. By the ruling's second call ("no syntax fault and no region depends on what a name names") an unknown unit is then no syntax fault and has no region; it is a fault of the stage that reads the vocabulary, and its literal a placeholder. That settles one edge of the §16 batch, if the owner agrees it follows. Not the other files of the package: ruled. Not the file's path: ids and spans are file-local, and the caller holds the path. Not a limit or an option: no production depends on one; the 2¹²⁷ literal is a check on a literal's digits, lexical or the evaluator's, and whether it is a syntax fault with a region stays with the §16 batch. Not a mode for the package file: ruled, package items parse in any file.

**Without a scan.** One rule covers all five: a bracket group is read once, left to right, into a list in scratch whose items each carry their class, and the construct the group is settled at the token after the closer, or for a brace group after `is` at the two tokens after it, tokens the pass reads anyway, so no token is read twice. Where the group stands is an argument the chooser passes to the machine (1.3): the same `(` is a lambda's start only where an argument or a `let`'s value starts, and a group everywhere else (a gate's `when (a):`, a `match (a):`, a `for` expression's `from (a):`):

- `(` where an argument or a `let`'s value starts: items are read as a name (fits a parameter and an expression), `Name ':' Type` (a parameter only) or any other expression (an expression only); at `)`, a `:` or `->` next makes a `Lambda` of the items, else exactly one expression-fitting item makes the group, and anything else is a fault at that token. `Name ':'` at the start is a lambda by its second token, as today.
- `(` where a type starts: items are `Name ':' Type` (a parameter only) or a type; at `)`, `->` next makes a `FnType`, else exactly one type makes the group.
- `Name [` in a type: the first list's items are read by the one machine as type-only, expression-only or both; a comma or a type-only item makes `TypeArgs`, `..` first makes `Dims`, and one item that fits an expression leaves the list neutral, handed on as (2) says.
- `Name [` in an expression: the same list; at `]`, `{` next makes a `RecordLit` with these as its `TypeArgs`, else the list is an `Index` (one item, or a bound form) and a type-only item or a comma is a fault at that token. This holds for the first list after the name only, since `RecordLit` takes one `TypeArgs`: `xs[0][1] {` is a fault at `{`.
- `{` after `is`: entries are read as `key ':' value` with each side's class; at `}`, `[` then `..` makes a `MapType` by §5.3's sentence, whose entries must fit types, else a `MapLit`, whose entries must fit expressions. `{` where a type argument starts is read the same way and stays neutral when `[..` follows, as (2) says. `{` elsewhere in an expression is a `MapLit`, and `Name {` a `RecordLit`, by §5.1's sentence "A `{` in an expression opens a map literal (§3.10), after a type name a record literal (§3.8), and after `json` a document literal (§3.11)".

The items live in scratch until the closer, as the trial's `Args` and `Array` keep theirs in `Scratch::parts`, and the node for the whole is pushed once, when its variant is known. No token is re-read, no `groups` map exists, and the hash operation at lookahead (the kept-code count's 2.2 row) goes with it.

## The tree and the views

**What the old tree is.** `Parsed` (`parsed.rs`) holds a copy of the file's text (`text: String`, a copy of source text against 2.2), a token table of kinds and starts (`token_columns.rs`), a tree of four columns indexed by node in completion order (`tree_columns.rs`: `kinds`, `first_tokens`, `end_tokens`, `first_descs`), the diagnostics list and two side lists (`detached`, `at_layout`), and three columns derived on first read behind `OnceLock` (`parents`, `token_parents`, `child_pool`). A node is its kind and its token range; a child is found by walking the range (`child_pool.rs`, `children.rs`), and a token's owner by range arithmetic. The 108 view files under `ast/` cast a node by kind and find a part by kind among the children (`support.rs`: `child`, `children`, `token`) or by position (`name_at(&self.0, 1)` for a placement's name, `placement.rs:38`, `wire_placement.rs:38`), with `_ =>` arms in 10 of them and a wrong answer in 5 (the conformance review's C1 to C5). The tree builder renames token kinds after the parse (`tree_builder.rs:33`). The kept-code count lists these against lines 1.1, 1.2, 1.5, 1.6 and 1.8.

**Three options.**

- T-A, keep and repair: keep the columns and the views, write the derived columns in the parser (one pass after the tree completes), replace `name_at` by kind-and-position-free reads where possible, drop the catch-all arms, remove the `semantics` imports. Win: the smallest diff, and the lossless walk and `token_at_offset` already exist. Cost: 1.8 cannot be met without a role per child, which the four columns do not record, so every view that takes the second `Ident` or the first `Expr` child keeps reading by position in another spelling; the text copy and the diagnostics list stay unless the result type is rewritten anyway; the views' 3,009 lines are kept for a consumer, the old IR build, that is itself replaced.
- T-B, reshape: keep a generic node table (kind, span) and add a role column, one small integer a node naming its role in its parent as the parent's production lists it; generate the views from one table of productions and roles, so a view finds a part by role. Win: a generic tree that a formatter or the editor walks without knowing every variant; the views made from one table, not by hand. Cost: the role is a number read against a per-kind table, 1.8's "no number means something by its range" in all but name; a new variant or a new role stops no build (1.6); two tables, the tree's and the roles', describe one thing; the generator is one more tool to keep.
- T-C, a typed node column (the pick): one variant a construct, each part a named field holding an id, a `TokId`, a `Parts` run in a side column, or an `Option` of one; nodes appended in completion order to dense columns, with a parallel span column; the token table beside them; the `Fault` as a variant; no separate views, because a view is a `match` on the variant and a part is a field. An id's type names the family of node it points at, so a read of a part is a direct index and never a `match` whose other arms are impossible states (1.11): either one column a family, the expressions and types, the body's items, the top level's items, the package file's, each with its own id type and its own enum, or one column with one accessor a family that routes the impossible variant through 1.11's one call, a few listed sites; the structure proposal chooses, and the row-size bound goes with the choice. Win: 1.8 by construction (a part is a field; a position is nowhere); 1.6 by construction (a `match` on a node names every variant, and a new variant stops the build at every question); 1.1 (no derived column, no renamed kind, nothing changed after it is written); 1.5 (no diagnostics list beside the tree; a fault is a node, the lexer's faults included, as (3) says); 2.2 (no copy of text, one row a node, no allocation a node once a column has room); the standard's own example and the trial `home.rs` are this form, so a builder has seen it. Cost: a generic walk, "the parts of this node in order", is one function beside each enum that names every variant, needed by the lossless check and by any tool; the row size is the largest variant's of its column, so a construct with many optional parts (a placement has seven) is split or its parts go through `Parts`, which the structure proposal decides under a stated bound; the editor's "which node holds this offset" is a halving search over the span column, and a parent column, if the editor's page wants one, is a table of its own written by one pass after the parse. And if the IR build is kept (Open: what "keep the IR" keeps), its reading side is rewritten against the new tree: at `6491d802`, 309 lines in 40 files under `semantics/ir/` (10,230 lines) name a view type, `NodeView` or `TokenView`, counted by `grep`; T-A alone keeps the interface they read, and even T-A changes the five wrong accessors' answers.

**The views, by hand or from a table.** Under T-C the question falls: there is no view to write; the enum is written by hand as the design, and its variants are the table. Under T-B the views are made from one table and never by hand. T-A keeps them by hand.

**Kept, reshaped or rewritten.** The tree: rewritten, as T-C. What carries over is the form of the columns (dense, indexed by id, in completion order), the token table of kinds and starts, the lossless check and the preorder walk as a check. The views: dropped, not rewritten, if the IR build is replaced; if the IR build is kept, its reading side is rewritten against the new tree, the 309 lines above, which is still less than keeping 3,009 lines of views whose answers 1.8 forbids. The lexer and the grammar: rewritten, ruled.
