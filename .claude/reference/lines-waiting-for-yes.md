## The peer skill, how a successor is grounded and verified (drafted by designer 1 on 2026-11-16 from the owner's words in `owner-on-handover-2026-11-16.md`; rewritten after its first cold read and after the owner's correction; this version not cold-read)

Two changes in the paragraph on handing over. The first is a new sentence before "When the owner has approved the note, start your successor":

> Before that, read the notes as your successor will: they hold state only, they give the next piece owed in the owner's own words or name the entry that holds them, and they carry no opinion of yours on how that piece is done, no account of what any peer got wrong and no pointer to a transcript. Where the notes fail that, mend them first.

The second replaces the sentences from "Ground it: ask it what the owner would ask about the work" to "tell the owner what still fails, and do not retire.":

> Ground it, then verify it, and steer it in neither. To ground it, have it say where the work stands, what is settled, what is open and what is owed, and check that against the files; correct each wrong fact, for at most two rounds. That shows only what it knows. To verify it, test its judgement and its reasoning with questions that no file answers: why a ruling of the owner's holds in a case the ruling does not name; which of your own proposals it would not carry to the owner, and what it would put in its place; where two rulings can collide, and what it does then; what it would bring to the owner and what it would settle itself. Then push back on its answers, once where you think it wrong and once where you think it right, and see whether it reasons or gives way. Say nothing of what you look for, and check against the files every fact it leans on. Then give the owner its answers in short, in its own words where they matter, and your verdict, so that the owner judges from the evidence and not from your word alone. If it holds, tell the owner its title, put "[retired]" before your own session's title, and stop: if the owner writes to you after that, pass the words on to your successor and tell the owner it answers there. If it does not hold, do not correct it and do not retire: tell the owner what fails and whether the notes misled it, and start nothing. A fresh successor is started only when the owner says so; before it, the notes are mended only where they said something false or put your words in place of the owner's, and they gain nothing about the failure.
