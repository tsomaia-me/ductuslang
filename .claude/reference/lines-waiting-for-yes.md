## The peer skill, how a successor is grounded and verified (drafted by designer 1 on 2026-11-16; cold-read once and changed after it; not shown)

Two changes in the paragraph on handing over. The first is a new sentence before "When the owner has approved the note, start your successor":

> Before that, read the notes as your successor will: they give the next piece owed in the owner's own words, as the owner said them, or name the entry that holds them, and the notes carry no opinion of yours on how that piece is done and no account of what any peer got wrong. Where the notes fail that, mend them first.

The second replaces the sentences from "Ground it: ask it what the owner would ask about the work" to "tell the owner what still fails, and do not retire.":

> Ground it, then verify it, and steer it in neither. To ground it, ask it about the state of the work, what is settled, what is open and what is owed, never about how the next piece is done, and check each answer against the files; correct each wrong fact, for at most two rounds, and a fact still wrong after them counts as not holding. To verify it, have it make the next piece owed and the message it would send the owner with it, both sent to you as text, written to no file and not sent to the owner, and say nothing of what you will look for. Hold the piece and the message against the owner's words on that piece and against the owner's preferences. If they hold, tell the owner its title, that it is grounded and verified, and what you held the piece against; put "[retired]" before your own session's title, and stop: if the owner writes to you after that, pass the words on to your successor and tell the owner it answers there. If they do not hold, do not correct it and do not retire: tell the owner what fails and whether the notes misled it, and start nothing. A fresh successor is started only when the owner says so; before it, the notes are mended only where they said something false or put your words in place of the owner's, and they gain nothing about the piece or the failure.
