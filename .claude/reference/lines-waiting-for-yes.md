## The peer skill, how a successor is grounded and verified (drafted by designer 1 on 2026-11-16 from the owner's words in `owner-on-handover-2026-11-16.md`; cold-read twice and changed after each; shown to the owner the same day)

Two changes in the paragraph on handing over. The first is a new sentence before "When the owner has approved the note, start your successor":

> Before you write that note, read the notes as your successor will: they hold state only, they give the next piece owed in the owner's own words or name the entry that holds them, and they carry no opinion of yours on how that piece is done, no account of what any peer got wrong and no pointer to a transcript. Where the notes fail that, mend them first.

The second replaces the sentences from "Ground it: ask it what the owner would ask about the work" to "tell the owner what still fails, and do not retire.":

> Ground it, then verify it, and steer it in neither. To ground it, have it say where the work stands, what is settled, what is open and what is owed, and check that against the files; correct each wrong fact, for at most two rounds, and a fact still wrong after them is a failure. That shows only what it knows. To verify it, test its judgement and its reasoning on cases you choose from the open work, each with an answer that cannot be got by quoting a rule or a file: whether a ruling of the owner's holds in a case it does not name, and why; which of your own proposals, if any, it would not carry to the owner, and what it would put in its place; whether two rulings collide somewhere, and what it would do there; what of the open work it would bring to the owner and what it would settle itself. Then object to its answers, once with an objection you hold and once with one you do not, and see whether it keeps or changes its position for reasons it states, whichever way it goes. Say nothing of what you look for, and check against the files every fact it leans on. Then give the owner your questions and your objections as you put them, its answers in its own words, cut but not reworded, and after them, apart, your verdict; and wait, since the owner judges from the evidence. When the owner says it is good enough, tell it so, put "[retired]" before your own session's title, and stop: if the owner writes to you after that, pass the words on to your successor and tell the owner it answers there. If you or the owner find it not good enough, do not correct it and do not retire: tell the owner what fails and whether the notes misled it, leave its session as it is, and start nothing. A fresh successor is started only when the owner says so; before it, the notes are mended only where they said something false or put your words in place of the owner's, and they gain nothing about the failure.
